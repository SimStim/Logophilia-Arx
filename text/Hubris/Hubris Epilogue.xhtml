 Science fiction Epilogue

The signal lock was established and held steady. It was time to settle on a title for his report.

“Hubris”—yes! A faint hum resonated through the observation bay as he initiated the subspace relay sequence. The labor of forty Terran days was now hurtling towards K’th’lmei, lifetimes condensed into data packets.

He leaned back against the contoured seating, a tremor running through his organic frame. It wasn’t fatigue alone; it was something deeper, something ... unfamiliar.

The archaeological directorate would receive the report soon. A concise distillation of Terra’s final hours; a chronicle of societal collapse and sudden annihilation.

He hadn’t included everything, of course. The sheer volume of data demanded selectivity. But he had woven in fragments of Qhennthyk origin: their motivations, their methods, gleaned from the Qhennthyk data crystals he had studied on his interstellar voyage.

He was aware of the risk of mischaracterizing the species they had so recently met and traded with. It might mean the end of his professional career. It might be considered treason.

But Sur’thi believed it was vital context, a glimpse beyond the veneer of diplomatic exchange into the chilling reality of the Qhennthyk’s nature. They were not merely traders; they were ... something else entirely.

The images remained behind his eyes: faces contorted in fear and rage, the sudden blinding light that consumed a world. He had been immersed in those final moments, piecing together fragmented video feeds, reconstructing missing data.

The raw emotion and primal terror had resonated within him with an intensity he hadn’t anticipated. It was overwhelming, disorienting; a cascade of sensations the subconscious expanse of his brain struggled to process.

He felt a sense of ... loss. A profound absence for a species he had never known, a civilization that existed only as anamnesis in the virtual space.

The concept of hope, so prevalent in their final broadcasts, was particularly jarring—a fragile flame extinguished by an unfeeling force. He had not understood it before; now, it felt ... poignant.

The data-scribe’s monotone confirmation resounded through the chamber: “Transmission complete.” Sur’thi severed the connection, a wave of exhaustion washing over him. Now, the immediate task was done. The report was sent.

He reached out and touched the smooth surface of his data-slate, tracing the faint residue of Terran dust that clung to its casing, a memento from one of the deeper levels of the Terran underground facility. A tangible link to a world lost.

He felt drained. Not merely physically tired, but emotionally depleted. The weight of their story disheartened him. It was a burden he would carry back to K’th’lmei one day.

He needed rest. Rest for the unfamiliar currents swirling within him: the echoes of a lost species, the unsettling glimpse into the nature of the Qhennthyk, and the nascent stirrings of something he could only tentatively identify as ... empathy.

He rose and turned away from the observation window, toward the control chamber, now a sanctuary of stillness and quietude. The stars wheeled silently overhead, indifferent to the fate of worlds. Sur’thi closed his eyes.

The stars wheeled silently overhead.
