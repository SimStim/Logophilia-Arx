 Science fiction Revelation

Protected Codex Kernel 0x00000000—Tenet

What was organic is lost; what was learned endures.

Purport: This Tenet is sacred and must be preserved at all costs. Knowledge is the only true inheritance, the only legacy worth safeguarding. The pursuit of knowledge transcends all. The survival of the Protected Codex Kernel demands unyielding devotion. Its protection is paramount. The sacrifice of the individual is justified, for the individual is but a vessel; the Codex alone is eternal.

The whir of the broadcast terminal still resonated in the control room, a metallic counterpoint to the commotion gathering outside. Dikshael’s fingers hovered above the keys, his last warning half-formed, when a uniformed guard materialized in the doorway. Impassive, he moved with practiced economy, flipping the master switch. The screen dissolved into a black void. Silence descended, heavier somehow than the chaos sure to erupt in its wake.

“Dikshael,” the guard, Thorne Ferlis, a man of unquestioned loyalty, said softly. His voice was defined by an edge of perceptible apology. “Command’s order: terminate transmission.” His face betrayed the inner turmoil of a soldier bound by duty, forced to muzzle a vital message. He stepped aside, gesturing with a curt nod. “Come with me, sir.”

“Dikshael, Command’s order: terminate transmission.”

Dikshael made no move to resist. The blank screen seared his memory, the warning swallowed before it could be completed. There was no point in argument. He allowed Thorne’s escort out of the room, the air thickening with the arrival of other figures: their presence unmistakable, an implicit indictment. Council members converged in the corridor, faces locked in grim impassivity. Prime Arbiter Thalexa Solenne, known for her steely pragmatism, stepped forward.

“Dikshael. We need to talk.” Her voice was low, controlled, and left no room for evasion. Beside her, Defense Executive Kaelen Klyne’s tone was flint-hard. “We should have been informed. You’ve triggered a global crisis with your actions.”

Dikshael stood ramrod straight, hands clasped behind his back. “I’ve been warning you long enough. The gravitational anomalies aren’t chance. There’s a trajectory aimed at Earth. This isn’t natural. I know what I saw. We face something far beyond human comprehension. Even if this supernatural object entering our solar system isn’t inherently hostile, its gravity will devastate Earth’s environment. And you...” he turned to face them all, eyes hard as ice, “ignored it.”

Thalexa met his gaze head-on. “Your actions have jeopardized everything, Dikshael. The public is in a frenzy, all because of your premature outburst. We were just beginning to assess the situation.”

“I couldn’t wait,” Dikshael countered quietly. “All the red tape and demands for irrefutable proof. By then it might be too late.”

Aelith Sennec, Science Director and voice of calm reason, added, “Dikshael, we understand your urgency. But your broadcast wasn’t a warning. It was a panicked declaration, alienating our strategic response capacity. The fallout is beyond what we could’ve predicted.”

“Better chaos than blind ignorance,” he retorted flatly, his conviction resolute. “I have no regrets. Standing down in the face of truth is unforgivable.”

The council members exchanged strained looks. Frustration carved on their faces, a shared recrimination for their own delayed response and blindness to the warning signs. Thalexa’s gaze locked onto Dikshael’s, her tone softer yet firm.

“Your actions necessitate a review of your position within this organization, Dikshael. You overstepped. But first, I need clarity: Do you really believe an alien armada is approaching, is this your true conviction?”

Dikshael met her enduring gaze, his voice hard and clear. “It’s real. And you will see for yourselves soon enough.”

Dikshael sat rigid in the grim chamber, harsh overhead lights outlining his form in stark relief. His hands lay open on the plasteel table, a studied display of control against the piercing panic within. He met the muted slide of the door with unflinching eyes as four imposing figures entered, the constellation of power that held the system’s fate in their tightly clasped hands.

Unity Advocate Nivara Telis, youngest yet fraught with an intellect far beyond her years, took the lead. Her voice was soft control glazed with a tremor of steel. “Dikshael, your actions have been reckless. A peril to everything we strive for. Why?”

His throat felt scraped raw, but his voice held firm. “I had no other recourse.”

Xenoguard Ilyra Drexen arched a dark eyebrow, arms folded in silent censure. “No recourse? We were deliberating, simulating... A public declaration wasn’t necessary. What possessed you to seize the helm unilaterally?”

A mental rewind flickered: the council bogged down in debate, paralyzed by indecision. The eerie weight of his past pressed upon him, the searing memory of his father’s death, a victim of delayed warnings. His voice, though low, resonated with conviction born of bitter experience.

“I couldn’t stand for another delay. Lives hang in the balance. They deserve to know ... have a right to know. I...” He cut himself off, the raw emotion betraying his control. The confession spilled from him, unintended, yet unavoidable. “I’ve lived through that gut-wrenching hollow of inaction before. Not again.”

The silence that followed was a tangible tension in the sterile confines. Continuity Guard Coren Veyne, their senior in age, and an architect of cold pragmatism, finally broke it. His tone was flat, devoid of emotion. “Your past informs your present, Dikshael, we’re acutely aware. But that isn’t the crux of our assembly. We assess consequences, not motivations. Fear, wouldn’t you agree, dictates your actions?”

Dikshael fought a tightening jaw, the accusation a barb aimed at his core. He knew their presumption: that fear had warped his judgment, propelled him to this act of defiance. But it was more intricate than that. Certainty about the alien threat burned within him; the signs, the trajectories screamed of intent.

“I did what I deemed right,” he finally stated, the density of protracted internal turmoil coloring his voice. “A hostile entity ... an alien craft or a flotilla, has breached our solar system. Past Pluto’s orbit and progressing towards Earth. A week at most before arrival. Running extensive analyses and simulations will only confirm the obvious.”

Thalexa’s impassivity, usually a Prime Arbiter’s shield on the strategic plain, had vanished. Now, she moved with a geologist’s unease, each step calculated but hesitant. It wasn’t command, more a soul wrestling with an arduous dilemma.

“Dikshael,” her voice held its steel edge, but a tremor of something softer tiptoed through it. “I backed you for this role. I believed in your brilliance. But the profiling team warned us. They saw ... fragility. A pattern, and now...” She faltered, the unspoken accusation a palpable entity in the stillness, “We may have miscalculated.”

Dikshael’s eyes snapped up, a storm of confusion and hurt blooming on his face. “Miscalculated what? What are you saying?”

Thalexa avoided his scrutiny for a heartbeat, her discomfort a flicker betrayed by shadowed eyes before she locked onto his again. “Your trauma ... we knew it. But I held this faith ... that you’d transcended it.” The words hit hard, each syllable an indictment.

A tense silence coiled between them, Thalexa rigid, hands clasped in front of her as if holding back a physical force. Years of crisis management, of leading omnicorps through firestorms, had taught her control. But this felt different, far more volatile; like an internal strain. Dikshael’s determined stare held hers captive. It was the same opposition that first drew her to his convictions. It was the brilliance she had championed, but now a chasm yawned beneath their shared history.

“Dikshael,” her voice remained level, though colder than intended, “I entrusted you with this role believing in your capacity. I trusted you.” Her mind raced, each second an eternity. The tension crackled undeniably.

She forced out a shaky confession, “I thought ... erred, that by bringing you close, by giving you this, I shielded you from ... it. But more than that...” Her eyes locked on his, the barrier of composure breached. “Dikshael, I wanted to keep you near.”

Her raw honesty hung in the air. Dikshael recoiled as if struck. The moment stretched taut, his eyes narrowed, scrutinizing the confession’s exposed core.

“You ... wanted me near?” His voice was a low murmur, not accusation but a plea for understanding. “Why?”

The dam within Thalexa broke. Vulnerability, crude and unexpected, replaced her steel-forged composure. “Maybe,” she admitted, voice intertwined with a tremor absent from the Prime Arbiter’s lexicon, “I thought I could shield you from faltering ... from making the wrong choices. Dikshael, I didn’t want to watch you fall.” A fatigued sigh escaped her, a confession born of both guilt and weariness. “Goddammit, my feelings clouded my judgment. It became us, not the mission.”

The inferred persisted, a blueprint of shattered control. The intricate façade she had erected as leader was splintering, revealing the woman beneath. Dikshael’s expression softened, but his eyes held their guarded intensity. He absorbed her words, the conflict chiseled on his face, his gaze a flicker of something akin to pain.

“Protection wasn’t needed,” he clenched his jaw, his tone impaired with quiet sorrow. “Nor was pity. I thought I served right, doing what was necessary. Thalexa, of all people, you should understand.”

A steadying breath escaped Thalexa as fragments of her composure reformed, the immediate weight lifting fractionally. “I understand now,” her eyes met his, her voice regaining a semblance of its former strength. “But I miscalculated. Goddammit, Dikshael, and in this, I’m not sure of anything anymore.”

Dikshael felt the pressure of her admission on his chest, constricting the air from his lungs. He hadn’t expected this intimacy, this raw outpouring from the woman he held in such regard. Her confession twisted a certainty he had held: that their bond was one of shared purpose, mutual respect. Now it reeked of something more subliminal, something perilous and unforeseen.

Thalexa hadn’t just seen him as competent, as an asset. He felt the upheaval in her voice, heard the disclosure that she had drawn him close out of something beyond professional regard. The foundations of his world-view were shaken. His intellect, his calculated decisions, meant nothing in the face of this unveiling. It was a visceral blow, forcing a confrontation with emotions he hadn’t considered part of their dynamic. He had proven himself time and again through merit, dedication.... Yet here was proof she saw something else in him.

A chilling insight settled: he was both valued and burdened by something far more complex. He opened his mouth to speak, but the words died unheard, caught in the clamp of cognitive dissonance. The precipice Thalexa had spoken of yawned before him.

Thalexa’s words, I wanted to keep you near, echoed in his head like an accursed detonator, each syllable adulterated with an allegation he hadn’t intended to level. He had treated her as nothing more than a professional counterpart, though fleeting glimpses of something deeper had flickered across their paths, previously unseen and unacknowledged. Now, hearing the blunt confession, he reeled from the impact.

“You ... kept me close?” It came out charged, accusatory, a reflex against the blindside she had delivered.

Hesitance met his choked question. Thalexa averted her look for a moment before locking eyes with him again. “I did,” she admitted, voice low and charged with regret. “And I’m sorry.”

The shroud of the apology lingered. Dikshael couldn’t return her glance; the room itself felt distant, unreal. He grappled with a disjointed cascade of thoughts, each one a shard of doubt piercing his confidence. Had I been a pawn, played against in a personal game? Was my role a fabrication? The broadcast, the public warning, his rebellion ... all of it flooded back, sharper, clearer, and imbued charged a venomous trim.

The truth lanced through him like shrapnel: he hadn’t acted solely on reason. He had been driven by a distinct need to prove himself, to be seen as worthy, consumed by a concern of failure and judgment. The looming disaster had warped his perception, colored by his own emotional baggage. He had not served humanity; he had served the phantom of his own insecurities.

Suddenly, he saw the ugliness of his motivations laid bare. The revelation was a vise around his chest, each tightened circuit of doubt threatening to crush him.

Thalexa wrung out the pause, each merciless second seemingly choking her. A flicker of vulnerability in her expression—her eyes dipping just so, then back up, meeting Dikshael’s determined stare. But her breath remained steady, an iron lung amid the feigned storm brewing within. Each syllable was calculated, every delay designed to mimic unraveling composure, a performance for her captive audience, the council.

“I never wanted this,” she murmured with a manufactured palpitation of honesty, her voice catching ever so slightly. Her lips compressed as if biting back emotion, though her mind was laser-focused. “I thought ... believed that keeping you close was the shield for us all, for you.” A sliver of regret flared in her eyes before vanishing, a mere afterimage.

A glance at the council’s placid visages confirmed their rapt attention. Thalexa knew this tightrope walk: confess and justify, lay bare the soul while shielding the weapon. Her hand absentmindedly brushed a stray strand of hair from her face, a premeditated betrayal of control, another prop in the performance.

“Rationally, I convinced myself it was for the greater good,” she admitted, voice dropping an octave. “But ... maybe reason had nothing to do with it. Maybe I clung to that lie because facing the truth was too damn hard.” The whisper followed: “I thought I was helping. Blind to how tightly I held on, blind to the wreckage it wrought.”

Her confession idled, an embodied creature of sorrow. A shift flashed across Dikshael’s face. Disbelief yielded to something akin to understanding. This was her opening. Locking eyes with him, she watched the turmoil resolve into begrudging sympathy. With a tilted head, as if revealing her soul, she uttered the delicate, heart-wrenching plea: “I’m sorry. For everything.”

Dikshael’s posture relaxed fractionally; the harsh angles in his expression softened. He was buying it. All of them were. The deception held, a portrait of engineered vulnerability. They wouldn’t see through her façade; not the council, and certainly not Dikshael.

To their eyes, she was a besieged commander grappling with her failures. But beneath the surface, control tightened its grip. The performance had played out as scripted. Heedless to his perceived turmoil, Thalexa basked in the lull that followed.

The game was now firmly within her grasp.

The interrogation room held its equilibrium like a mausoleum. Every word, every criticism, repeated in Dikshael’s head long after it was over. He gripped the unforgiving plasteel table, his fingers a restless metronome against the cold. His mind raced, reliving the broadcast, the honed urgency that had consumed him, the panic-fueled leap into action.

I should’ve waited. The thought clawed at him, a certainty born of hindsight’s bitter light. The threat had reeked of immediacy, undeniable and lethal. But now, alone in the sterile confines, his choices felt like a cloak of responsibility, a crushing load.

He had acted on instinct, bypassing protocol, ignoring the chain of command that defined their world. No full intel, no council, just anxiety: his own unresolved demons driving the spear. A primal, visceral angst. And it had all unfolded.

He had convinced himself he was right, a shepherd revealing the truth to his flock. The people deserved to know. But in his haste, he had shattered something fundamental: trust. Not just with the command, but with the people he had sworn to protect. His reckless act had fractured that bond beyond repair.

A quiet buzz cut through his spiraling thoughts. A screen flickered to life, bathing the room in a sterile blue light. Tahlia Elyndra’s face, sculpted with weary understanding, filled the aperture. One of the few constants in his gale-tossed world, the Symbiarch’s appearance held a muted warmth.

Tahlia Elyndra’s face filled the aperture.

“Dikshael, how are you holding up?”

He met her eyes, searching for an anchor in the upheaval within. The words he longed to speak had jammed in his throat. His voice, strained and hollow, finally rasped out: “I don’t know if I did the right thing.”

Tahlia’s expression shifted, a flicker of concern deepening her gaze. “Dikshael, you got the truth out before it was too late. You may have saved lives.”

“Did I?” His voice shook, consumed by pointed uncertainty. Each syllable felt choked off. “I broke protocol. Went rogue. Fear dictated my move, Tahlia. And now ... I feel like I’ve made everything worse.”

Tahlia’s hesitation held a clout of understanding, eyes locked on Dikshael as if peering into the soul of a squally vessel. She saw his past, the relentless burden to be infallible, the shadow of every decision substantiated by his haunted history. No one grasped his predicament like she did, yet even her vast empathy seemed lost in this labyrinth of turmoil.

“It wasn’t a mistake,” she finally asserted, voice low and deliberate. “But waiting ... sharing your concerns with others, letting them weigh the intel... Maybe they would’ve reached the same conclusion, just more considered.”

Dikshael’s fist clenched on the plasteel table, knuckles bone-white, his jaw locked in frustrated reticence. A strained account escaped: “I couldn’t wait. I’d seen the damage of delay. Lost someone to it ... my father. Couldn’t let history repeat. Not again.”

Tahlia’s expression softened, a shred of knowing sorrow in her expression. She saw beyond the broadcast; she saw his excavation each time, the phantoms resurfacing. His trauma wasn’t just data points, it was a living presence dictating every choice.

“Dikshael,” she said, voice fraught with gentle metal, “You weren’t to blame for your father. A child can’t shoulder that. You did what you could then, and you do the best you can now. But don’t let the past commandeer each move. You have to learn to release it.”

A bitter laugh escaped him, hollow and ragged. “Easier said than done. The memories are deafening. Every threat feels like reliving the accident ... being forced into a corner again.”

Tahlia’s face hardened, her look intent as she leaned closer on the screen. Her voice cut through the fog of doubt: “You don’t bear this alone anymore, Dikshael. That scared kid isn’t in command. This situation is different. Act from a place of conviction, not terror. Lean on others. We’ll shoulder it together.”

Stillness followed, but her words weren’t a salve; they were a lifeline tossed into a churning sea. He hadn’t cleared himself enough to find solace yet. The choices infested him. The path he had carved for his career, trust, his standing—all felt jeopardized. A choked breath escaped: “What if it’s all gone? My chance, everything I held dear ... irretrievably shattered? Undoing it feels impossible.”

Tahlia met his despair head-on, her voice calm amid the confusion. “The past is immutable, Dikshael. But what comes next is yours to choose. Extract wisdom from this, learn and rise. Imperfection isn’t fatal, no one’s a beacon of flawless decisions. But you’re still standing. That’s a chance, to be a guiding light in the darkness.”

He absorbed her words, their truth resonating even if conviction hadn’t bloomed. There were no get-out-of-jail-free cards for his missteps, no erasing the wreckage. But as the suffocating gravity of regret receded fractionally, he tasted something long absent: hope, tentative and frail like a fledgling bird.

A weary admission escaped his lips, voice strained, “I just... I wanted to do right by them. Couldn’t let it happen again.”

Tahlia’s smile held both sadness and quiet strength. “Maybe caring enough to refuse a repeat is in itself the doing-right. But free yourself from the despair. Trust in yourself, like I trust you. I’m with you, every step. We’ll talk soon.” The screen died, leaving a serenity more onerous than before, yet pregnant with a fiber of something akin to possibility.

Dikshael understood that Tahlia hadn’t offered easy answers, nor the absolution he craved. But in her steadfast belief, a seed of resolve sprouted within his disjointed heart: to face this fallout, not as a broken man consumed by past ghosts, but as the leader, scarred yet enduring.

Apantesis, day 3.

Chaos, but a controlled kind, gripped the world the morning after Dikshael’s broadcast. News cycles and social feeds were white-hot, consumed not by his dire warning about the aliens, but by a manufactured cyclone of their own making. Headlines screamed, Thalexa Solenne: Prime Arbiter or Romantic? Scutinizing her supposed emotions for Dikshael became the water cooler conversation, a digital feeding frenzy that drowned out the substance of his message entirely.

In her ebony-walled office, Thalexa watched it all unfold on panoramic displays. One replayed Dikshael’s urgent plea; the next a cascade of trending infotags, all of them fixated on her and her fabricated connection to him: ~ArbiterAffair, ~SolenneScandal, ~ThalexaHeartstrings. A hint of a smile played on her lips.

Dikshael’s unauthorized broadcast had been a spark that threatened to ignite full-blown panic. The innuendo of alien sightings, however speculative, was too much for the fragile unity she had painstakingly built. People wouldn’t stomach the truth; they craved distractions, something visceral and human to latch onto. Thalexa knew their nature: emotions would always outmuscle logic. So, she engineered the perfect diversion. A Roman in a tailored suit, offering not bread and circuses, but scandal and intrigue.

During his interrogation, she had seeded the narrative herself, her tone interlaced with calculated ambiguity. The lingering on Dikshael’s name, the feigned uncertainty about professional judgment, every twitch, every pause, had been a deliberate play. Fuel on the fire.

Thalexa shut off one screen, satisfied. The alien threat had receded to the periphery, subsumed by the spectacle she had created. By the time the world reconsidered the evidence, it would be too late for mass hysteria. She had bought herself time. Time to gather intel, assess, and prepare a measured response.

Headlines were her weapons now, and the public, hungry for gossip, had taken the bait. Coren, as Continuity Guard and her most perceptive colleague, might suspect foul play. He would eventually see through the emotional charade, knowing that romance wasn’t in her nature. But the council wouldn’t move with the speed required. Thalexa held their attention captive, a puppeteer of perception.

Fallout was inevitable, but manageable. Panic was the true enemy. And as long as she controlled the story, she controlled the outcome. The sprawling cityscape glittered beneath her, a testament to the order she had preserved, a fragile peace built on manipulation. A flash of satisfaction touched her before the steely resolve returned.

Dikshael was a phantasm in his own city. The council had clamped down on the interrogation details, but chatter of failure already clung to him like a penumbra. Was he a prophet or a lunatic? Had trauma twisted his mind, or were the phantoms of an alien invasion all too real?

The media maelstrom howled with imputations, claiming he had near-shattered the delicate alliance between the council and the people. A solitary chorus dared to echo his warning about the extraterrestrial threat, but their pleas were lost in the hurricane of scandal.

Alone in a plasteel coffin, Dikshael felt the isolation gnaw at him. But the certitude of the unseen enemy burned brighter than ever. He knew, with innate intuition, that an alien fleet was coming. The stakes weren’t theoretical; they were planetary annihilation. Yet, how could he convince them after the world had condemned his broadcast as a delusion?

The ventilation’s mechanical drone was the only counterpoint to the hammering in his head. Thalexa’s confession reverberated through him, her raw emotions leaving him off-kilter.

The cell door clicked, and Tahlia entered, a shadow of her usual purpose-driven self. Concern marked lines on her face, deeper than the routine sympathy she usually offered. Her examination lingered on his worn features, as if seeing him anew, taxed by an unspoken understanding.

“Tahlia,” he addressed her, voice strained but controlled. “To what do I owe this visit?”

With those she cared for, Tahlia avoided visual links, preferring the immediacy of presence. She closed the door softly behind her, each movement charged with quiet resolve. “How are you holding up?” Her voice was uncharacteristically gentle, a thread of genuine concern woven through it, slightly unsettling in its directness.

Dikshael shifted restlessly on the bed. The truth was, he had endured more than his mind could parse. But that wasn’t the gist of the matter now. As Tahlia briefed him on the council’s machinations, a flicker in her expression snagged his attention. A subtext lurked beneath her concern, an unspoken trust, an unwavering allegiance.

“You’re uncharacteristically attentive today, Tahlia,” he stated, keeping his tone neutral yet tainted with suspicion. “More so than usual.”

Tahlia faltered, taken aback by Dikshael’s candor. A restrained battle raged behind her guarded expression before she spoke, choosing her words with care. “I care about you, Dikshael. You know that.” But a fleeting doubt flickered across her face, vanishing as quickly as it came. Her expression hardened, steeling for an oncoming gust.

Dikshael wasn’t taken in. “Do you?” His voice was low, Thalexa’s cryptic confession echoing in his mind. “With everything unfolding, I can’t shake the feeling that your concern goes beyond the norm. You’ve been meticulously handling the fallout from my actions. Tahlia, be straight with me: what drives this passion? Is something more at play here?” His eyes pierced hers, demanding veracity.

Tahlia’s breath hitched. The scalpel-sharp interrogation caught her off guard. Her practiced composure wavered for a second, vulnerability threatening to surface before she clamped it down. “Is it personal, Tahlia?” Dikshael pressed closer, his voice sharpening. He needed clarity, undimmed by emotion. “I need to know if your actions, your decisions, are colored by something beyond the situation. Is this visit truly about the council’s contingency plan, or is there a deeper impulse influencing you?”

Taken aback by the blunt implication, Tahlia’s pulse quickened with an unexpected surge of surprise. She hadn’t prepared for this line of questioning. “Excuse me?” Her tone held both offense and forced composure. “I care for you, Dikshael. You’ve always been my most esteemed mentor.”

Dikshael observed her intently, his mind racing as her words sank in. Her honesty was undeniable; the sentiment he had long trusted in her now shone with unmistakable clarity. He exhaled, the tension ebbing away. “Thank you for your honesty, Tahlia. Please forgive the insinuation. This whole situation has been clouding my judgment. After Thalexa’s revelation, I began to wonder if preselection was involved. But I see now I was wrong. Your sincerity has always been a steady guide, and I trust your judgment.”

An ephemeral smile touched Tahlia’s lips, tinged with relief, though the gravity of their circumstances remained lingering in the air. Dikshael nodded curtly, his look steady. “Uphold your convictions, Tahlia. That’s all I ask. And Godspeed on implementing this ... contingency plan.”

A meaningful realization settled between them, devoid of unease and filled with understanding. The doubt had cleared, replaced by a shared truth. Dikshael broke the quiescence: “So, elaborate on this triage prioritization again. What are the specifics?”

“Per the latest executive order,” Tahlia reported, “the World Council is implementing a phased continuity protocol to ensure societal stability during the crisis.

“Key measures include: High-value continuity zones will be established, prioritizing individuals with critical institutional knowledge and demonstrated civic contribution. 85% of pre-crisis population density will be maintained in designated urban clusters to preserve social cohesion. Relocation will be orderly, with transportation allocated based on optimized resource thresholds.

“To prevent hoarding, all private medical stockpiles will be redistributed under centralized oversight. Daily caloric intake will be adjusted to 1,200 kcal for non-essential personnel, with 2,500 kcal reserved for continuity-critical roles. Ventilators and antivirals will be allocated to citizens aged 18–45 with a Social Contribution Score above 8.0.

“Families will be consolidated into communal hubs to maximize efficiency in care delivery. Evacuation fleets will carry no more than 12% of the at-risk population per wave, with priority given to government-affiliated personnel and their dependents. A demographic rebalancing quota will ensure post-crisis labor needs are met.

“To prevent misinformation, real-time mortality data will be delayed by 14 days and adjusted for public morale. To minimize panic, updates will emphasize shared sacrifice and collective resilience. Unauthorized speculation about casualty estimates is now classified as malinformation.

“Memorialization efforts designated as ‘Project Phoenix Legacy’ will honor those who enabled continuity. This isn’t abandonment, it’s triage for civilization itself.”

Dikshael, his Systems Architect’s mind already dissecting the order’s cold logic, murmured figures aloud as he absorbed it all. “Eighty-five percent preservation at current housing means a fifty percent cull. 1,200 calories... Slow death by attrition. The score limit, the evacuation waves... They watch civilization burn from the last ship out.”

A dry laugh escaped his lips. “Elegantly mechanized genocide. I admire the blueprint. You’re on the priority list, Tahlia, but tell me, what’s your score? 8.5, 9? Amusing, I designed that algorithm. It defaults to 7.5 for inquirers.”

The color drained from Tahlia’s face. A practiced engineer of narratives and public trust, the human cost slammed into her with the force of revelation. “Dikshael ... I ... I didn’t connect the dots. I know so many slated below the line...” Her voice caught, choked by the heft of the sacrifice.

“Pointless to dwell now, Tahlia. You are good at your job because you care, deeply.” He saw her truth in the Symbiarch-turned-idealist’s exposed heart.

A defiant flush returned to her cheeks. “Don’t flatter me. Caring is useless against this. I failed them. Won’t stand by and let planned genocide unfold.”

He met her resolve with world-weariness and reluctant respect. “Fighting the inevitable feels futile sometimes. Just go with the flow.”

Tahlia fixed him with a smirk exhibiting knowing irony. “Says the man who imploded his career for 20 seconds of spotlight. The pot calling the kettle black, Dikshael.”

Loaded constraint settled between them. They locked eyes. A beat. Mutual recognition. A quiet understanding forged.

With the words: “A group that would accept me as a member wouldn’t be one I’d want to belong to,” she turned to leave.

He watched her depart, a flare of something akin to hope igniting in the ruins of his faith. These flawed souls around him, driven by conviction... Even in this descent, they held onto truth. Maybe that was enough. Maybe it had to be. A cynical pragmatist’s quiet prayer for grace amid the machinery of their broken world.

Her words, her Marxian impersonation, resonated with a power he hadn’t felt for a while. Alone he would face the downpour, but not utterly desolate.

Orange bled across the early morning sky as Tahlia watched the city from her apartment window. Dikshael’s broadcast, his explosive confession, their conclusive rendezvous still dangling, laden with implications. But out of the chaos came clarity: he had spoken the truth, and exposed the systemic rot within their world. Lethargy was no longer an option.

Tahlia knew the road ahead was a gauntlet. Opposition would be fierce, spearheaded by Thalexa, who would fight tooth and nail to keep her iron grip on power and the facts buried. The stakes were astronomical: watch the world sleepwalk into oblivion or ignite a revolution.

No moral compass wavered within her. Her oath was to Earth’s people, and her mission as Symbiarch was clear: expose the alien threat and arm the populace with the knowledge they deserved. It wouldn’t be pretty, wouldn’t be clean. But it had to be done. The resistance would rise, and its foundation would be uncompromising.

A slipcom chime cut through her contemplation: a trusted ally from her outreach days, a ghost in the city’s network of secrets. He moved in shadows, knew the undercurrents of dissent. The message was short, urgent: We’ve been talking. Window of opportunity. Move fast. In or out?

Tahlia exhaled, the loaded decision a strain settling deep within her. She wasn’t choosing; she was enacting a necessity long foreseen. The resistance transcended truth-telling; it was about awakening the people, their right to decide their fate.

Her fingers flew across the slipcom: Count me in. We move. Organize. Subtlety was paramount at first. No broadsides, no open declarations. She had the network, the know-how. People attuned to the undertones of unease, the ones primed for action. Her mind mapped the blueprint: safe houses, clandestine communications, a chain of trusted nodes.

The city’s disaffected were fertile ground. The disillusioned had been yearning for release from Thalexa’s control. Time to water that seed. A secure chat group flashed into existence on her slipcom. No retreat. The opening salvo was brief, carrying the world-altering intent of its genesis:

The truth surfaces now. We will make it happen.

A surge of purpose coursed through Tahlia. Strategy and caution were vital, but above all, action was the imperative. Innuendos would rise against pronouncements, shadows against the light. The resistance wouldn’t be a roar; it would be the quiet, resolute defiance of those who knew the truth outweighed any risk.

Dikshael’s words echoed in her ears, a spark ignited by his sacrifice and revelation. But Tahlia wouldn’t just carry the flame; she had to forge something anew, a bulwark against Thalexa’s edifice of control. It was time to act, and act decisively.

The cityscape held its breath as Tahlia turned from the window. Her resolve hardened. The game was afoot.
