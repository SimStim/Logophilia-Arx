 Non-fiction Trivia
Mathematics

−40 degrees Fahrenheit is equal to −40 degrees Celsius, which is a unique point where the two scales coincide.

A baker’s dozen traditionally consists of 13 items instead of the usual 12, giving customers an extra item.

Multiplying 111,111,111 by itself results in a palindrome: 111,111,111 × 111,111,111 = 12,345,678,987,654,321.

The sum of the first n odd numbers is n2. For example, the sum of the first 3 odd numbers (1, 3, 5) is 1 + 3 + 5 = 9 = 32.

The difference between the squares of consecutive numbers n and n + 1 is always equal to 2n + 1. For example, 42 − 32 = 16 − 9 = 7 = 2 × 3 + 1.

The digital root of a number is the single digit obtained by iteratively summing its digits until a single digit is left. For example, the digital root of the number 12,345 is 6 because 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 equals 15 and then 1 + 5 equals 6. This has applications in verifying the accuracy of calculations.

To multiply any two-digit number n by 11, add its two digits and place the sum between them. If the sum is two digits, carry the tens digit. For example, 11 × 27 = 297, and 11 × 48 = 528 (4 + 8 = 12, therefore place 2 between 4 and 8, then carry the tens digit of 12 to make 528).

The square of any two-digit number n ending in 5 can be found by multiplying the first digit of n by the next consecutive number and appending 25. For example, 652 = (6 × 7) with 25 appended gives 4225, and 952 = (9 × 10) with 25 appended gives 9025.

The opposite faces of a standard six-sided die always add up to 7 (1 + 6, 2 + 5, 3 + 4).

The sum of the first n powers of 2 is 2n + 1 − 1. For example, 20 + 21 + 22 = 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 = 23 − 1.

Multiplying any number by 9 and then summing the digits of the result yields 9. For instance, 9 × 73 = 657, and 6 + 5 + 7 = 18, and 1 + 8 = 9.

A self-descriptive number is an integer m of length b in base b, where each digit d at position n (starting from zero) counts how many times digit n appears in m. For example, in base 10, the number 6210001000 is self-descriptive because:

  • It has 10 digits, matching its base.
  • The digits indicate:
  • 6 zeros,
  • 2 ones,
  • 1 two,
  • 0 threes,
  • 0 fours,
  • 0 fives,
  • 1 six,
  • 0 sevens,
  • 0 eights,
  • 0 nines.

The number 153 is a magic number because it equals the sum of its own digits each raised to the power of the number of digits, specifically 13 + 53 + 33 = 153. This property defines it as an Armstrong number.

The Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ...) appears in the arrangement of petals in flowers and the branching of trees.

The Four Color Theorem states that any map can be colored using at most four colors in such a way that no two adjacent regions have the same color. This was proven in 1976 with the help of computers.

Pierre de Fermat famously claimed to have a proof that an + bn = cn has no non-zero integer solutions for n > 2. This was finally proven by Andrew Wiles in 1994 after more than 350 years.

The Birthday Paradox: In a group of just 23 people, there is a better than even chance that at least two of them share the same birthday.

Contrary to common belief, infinity (∞) is not a number but a concept representing something that is unbounded or limitless.

Prime numbers, though appearing randomly distributed, exhibit patterns such as twin primes (pairs like 11 and 13 that differ by 2) and prime quadruplets occurring in specific sequences. Additionally, primes in close proximity tend to avoid having the same last digit. Research in 2016 by two mathematicians revealed that a prime ending in 1 is less likely than expected to be followed by another prime also ending in 1, contrary to random sequences.

The Banach–Tarski Paradox states that a solid ball can be decomposed into a finite number of pieces, which can then be rearranged using only rotations and translations to form two identical copies of the original ball.

Euler’s identity (also known as Euler’s equation) ei × π + 1 = 0, is often considered one of the most beautiful equations in mathematics. It combines five fundamental mathematical constants (e, i, π, 1, and 0) using addition and exponentiation.

The Koch Snowflake is a fractal curve constructed by starting with an equilateral triangle and replacing the middle third of each line segment with two segments forming an equilateral bump. It has a finite area but infinite perimeter.

Mathematician Kurt Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem asserts that within any formal system of mathematics, there exist true statements that cannot be proven true using the rules and axioms of that system.

The Collatz Conjecture, often referred to as the “3n + 1 problem,” posits that beginning with any positive number and following two rules (if even, divide by two; if odd, triple it and add one) will inevitably result in reaching the number one.

A Möbius strip, discovered by Listing and Möbius in 1858 and seen in ancient Roman mosaics, is a surface with only one side and one edge, formed by joining a strip of paper with a half-twist. It’s non-orientable, meaning directions like clockwise and counterclockwise can’t be distinguished inside it. Every non-orientable surface includes a Möbius strip.

Origins of zero: Ancient Egyptian numerals were base 10 and used non-positional hieroglyphs. A papyrus from 1770 BC records the pharaoh’s court finances, using the nfr hieroglyph to denote equal amounts received and disbursed. Egyptologist Alan Gardiner suggested nfr symbolized zero. This symbol also marked the base level in tomb and pyramid drawings, with distances measured above or below it.

The digits of π are infinite and non-repeating. It’s known to millions of decimal places, but it never settles into a pattern.

Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) was a self-taught Indian mathematician who made significant contributions to mathematical analysis, number theory, infinite series, and continued fractions. Despite limited formal training, he independently developed nearly 3,900 results, many of which were novel and inspired further research. His correspondence with G.H. Hardy led to his travel to Cambridge, where his groundbreaking theorems gained recognition. Ramanujan became one of the youngest Fellows of the Royal Society and the first Indian Fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge. He had incredible intuitive insight into numbers, often arriving at results without formal proofs. His work, including the Ramanujan prime and theta functions, continues to influence mathematics.

A perfect number is a positive integer equal to the sum of its proper divisors. The first perfect number is 6 (1 + 2 + 3).

Numbers like π and e are transcendental, meaning they are not the root of any non-zero polynomial equation with rational coefficients.

The largest known prime number is 282,589,933 − 1, which has 24,862,048 digits in base 10. It was discovered in 2018 by a computer volunteered by Patrick Laroche through the Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search (GIMPS).

A Platonic solid is a convex, regular polyhedron in three-dimensional space, with congruent regular polygonal faces and the same number of faces meeting at each vertex. There are only five such polyhedra: Tetrahedron (4 faces), Cube (6 faces), Octahedron (8 faces), Dodecahedron (12 faces), and Icosahedron (20 faces). Now you know why the 1d10 die used in tabletop games looks out of place. Next time, ask your GM to avoid using it.

The integers 23 and 32 are two perfect powers (powers with an exponent greater than one) of natural numbers whose values (8 and 9, respectively) are consecutive. In 2002, Preda Mihăilescu proved that this is the only instance of two consecutive perfect powers.

Benford’s Law: In many naturally occurring datasets, the leading digit is more likely to be small (e.g., 1 appears as the first digit about 30% of the time).

The P versus NP problem is a major unsolved issue in theoretical computer science. It asks whether every problem whose solution can be quickly verified can also be quickly solved.

The frequencies of musical notes follow a mathematical ratio. For example, an octave is a doubling of frequency, so if the note A is 440 Hz, the next A is 880 Hz. In music theory, the 1-4-5 chord progression (e.g., C-F-G in the key of C major) is widely used in countless songs across various genres for its harmonic stability and familiarity.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, mathematical models predicted infection peaks and guided public health responses, such as social distancing measures and lockdowns. The SIR model (Susceptible, Infected, Recovered) helped predict COVID-19 spread, influencing vaccination strategies and lockdown policies.

Auction design, such as in Google’s ad auction system, uses game theory to ensure that advertisers bid truthfully for ad placements.

The artist M.C. Escher used tessellations to create interlocking and repeating patterns.

The Borda count, used in some political elections, ranks candidates by assigning points based on position on each ballot, ensuring a more representative outcome.

Amazon uses linear programming to optimize delivery routes, reducing shipping costs and delivery times. Linear programming (LP), or linear optimization, is a method for finding the best outcome (like maximum profit or minimum cost) in a mathematical model defined by linear relationships. It is a specific type of mathematical programming, also known as mathematical optimization.

Quantitative analysts use stochastic models to predict stock price movements, enabling high-frequency trading strategies that capitalize on small price changes. The Black-Scholes model helps in pricing options, allowing investors to hedge against market volatility.

JPEG compression reduces image file sizes by transforming pixel data using the discrete cosine transform (DCT), making images more manageable for storage and transmission.

Meteorologists use numerical models that solve complex equations to predict weather patterns, enabling accurate storm warnings.

In baseball, sabermetrics (the statistical analysis of baseball data) uses statistics to evaluate player performance, influencing team strategies and player trades.

CT scans use the Radon transform (introduced in 1917 by Johann Radon) to reconstruct images from X-ray data, providing detailed views of internal organs.

The design of the Sydney Opera House involved complex calculations to optimize acoustics, ensuring clear sound for the audience.

Traffic engineers use mathematical models to design signal timings at intersections, reducing congestion and improving traffic flow.

In topology, knot theory studies mathematical knots, inspired by everyday knots like those in shoelaces and ropes. However, a mathematical knot differs because its ends are joined, making it impossible to untie. The simplest knot is a ring, also known as the “unknot.” Knot theory helps biologists understand how DNA strands unknit and recombine during replication and repair, crucial for genetic research.

The Hodgkin–Huxley model, also known as the conductance-based model, is a mathematical framework that explains the initiation and propagation of action potentials in neurons. This model comprises nonlinear differential equations that simulate the electrical properties of excitable cells, including neurons and muscles. As a continuous-time dynamical system, it plays a crucial role in understanding neurological phenomena and developing treatments for disorders such as epilepsy.
