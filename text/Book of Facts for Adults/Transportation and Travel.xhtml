 Non-fiction Trivia
Transportation and Travel

In 2019, the Last Overland Expedition completed its 19,000 km journey, arriving in the UK. Traversing 23 countries from Singapore to London, the eight-member team successfully recreated the historic Oxford & Cambridge Far Eastern Expedition of 1955–56. This constitutes the world’s longest road trip.

The longest continuous spaceflight by a human is 437 days, achieved by Russian cosmonaut Valeri Polyakov in 1994–1995 aboard the Mir space station.

The longest non-stop commercial flight is from Singapore to New York, covering over 9,537 miles and taking 18 hours and 40 minutes.

The Peel P50, produced in the 1960s, is the smallest car ever made, at just 54 inches long and 41 inches wide. Designed as a city car, it was advertised to seat “one adult and a shopping bag.” It featured a single door on the left side, one windscreen wiper, and a single headlight.

Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport has consistently held the title of the world’s busiest airport by passenger numbers every year except 2020 for more than two decades.

The Titanic was discovered in 1985, 73 years after it sank, by a joint French-American expedition led by Jean-Louis Michel and Robert Ballard. The team was originally on a mission to locate two Cold War-era nuclear submarines.

The Maglev train in Shanghai holds the record for the fastest commercial train speed, reaching 431 km/h (268 mph).

On May 20–21, 1927, Charles Lindbergh made the first solo nonstop transatlantic flight from New York to Paris, taking 33.5 hours.

The Gotthard Base Tunnel in Switzerland is the world’s longest railway and deepest traffic tunnel, measuring 35.5 miles (57.09 km) in length.

Trieste, a Swiss-designed, Italian-built bathyscaphe, became the first crewed vessel to reach Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench in 1960. This achievement was the culmination of Project Nekton, a series of dives by the US Navy near Guam. Piloted by Swiss oceanographer Jacques Piccard and US Navy lieutenant Don Walsh, Trieste reached a depth of about 10,916 meters (35,814 feet), a record that remains unchallenged.

The oldest operating ferry service is the “Maid of the Mist,” which has been transporting passengers near Niagara Falls since 1846.

According to some sources, the first recorded automobile accident occurred in 1891 in Ohio City, Ohio. The incident involved a Buckeye gasoline buggy, one of the first American gas-powered vehicles. Designed by John William Lambert, the car hit a tree root, veered off course, and collided with a hitching post.

The Highway 1 in Australia is the longest national highway in the world, stretching over 14,500 km (9,000 mi) around the entire country.

The Embraer Phenom 100 is one of the smallest commercial jets, capable of seating 4 passengers and flying over 1,350 miles.

The Gossamer Albatross, a human-powered aircraft, flew across the English Channel on June 12, 1979, achieving a distance of 22.5 miles.

The first GPS satellite was launched in 1978, and the 24 satellite system was fully operational by 1993, revolutionizing navigation.

Dennis Tito, an American businessman, became the first space tourist in 2001, paying $20 million for a trip to the International Space Station.

MSC’s 6 Irina-class of ships, built in 2023, are the largest cargo ships in the world by capacity, able to carry 24,346 containers.

The ancient Silk Road facilitated trade and cultural exchange between China and the Mediterranean, spanning over 4,000 miles (6,400 km) and connecting civilizations for centuries.

The first practical steam locomotive, invented by George Stephenson in 1814 and named Blücher, transformed transportation by enabling faster and more efficient movement of goods and people.

The Panama Canal, completed in 1914, revolutionized maritime trade by creating a shortcut between the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, reducing travel distances for ships. The American Society of Civil Engineers has ranked the Panama Canal one of the Seven Wonders of the Modern World.

The Concorde was the world’s first and only successful supersonic passenger airliner, capable of speeds over Mach 2 (1,354 mph or 2,180 km/h) and seating 92 to 128 passengers. It entered service in 1976 and operated for 27 years.

The Trans-Siberian Railway, completed in 1916, spans 5,772 miles (9,289 km) across Russia, connecting Moscow to Vladivostok. It is still the longest railway line in the world.

The London Underground, opening on January 10, 1863, is the world’s oldest metro system, serving millions daily with its extensive network of tunnels and iconic Tube map design.

India’s expansive rail network, one of the world’s busiest, to this day includes “toy trains” in mountainous regions like Darjeeling, offering scenic journeys with narrow gauge tracks and historic steam engines.

Vaporetti are water buses in Venice, essential for public transportation along the Grand Canal and across the lagoon, providing locals and tourists with a unique mode of travel amid historic architecture.

The San Francisco cable car system, the world’s last manually operated cable car system, is an iconic symbol of the city. Listed on the National Register of Historic Places, it is one of only two street railways designated as a National Historic Landmark, alongside New Orleans’ St. Charles Streetcar Line. Originally, 23 lines were established between 1873 and 1890, but only three remain today.

Samurai walking paths, such as the Nakasendo in Japan, were historic routes used by feudal lords and samurai for travel between Kyoto and Edo (Tokyo), featuring post towns and preserved cultural sites.

The Interrail Pass allows travelers to explore Europe by train, offering unlimited travel across multiple countries within a set period, promoting cultural exchange and discovery through rail travel.
