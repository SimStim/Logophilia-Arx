 Non-fiction Trivia
Physics

Light can be slowed down. In 1999, scientists at Harvard University slowed down light to 38 miles per hour by passing it through a Bose–Einstein condensate.

Particles can pass through barriers that they seemingly shouldn’t be able to, a phenomenon known as quantum tunneling.

The lowest temperature possible, absolute zero (−273.15°C or −459.67°F), is a point where atomic motion nearly ceases.

Predicted by Einstein in 1916 and first directly observed in 2015, gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime caused by accelerating masses, like merging black holes.

Einstein’s famous equation E=mc2 shows that mass and energy are interchangeable, leading to the development of nuclear energy and weapons.

Neutrinos are nearly massless particles and pass through Earth by the trillions every second without interacting with matter.

The light particle, photon, has no mass and travels at the speed of light, which is about 299,792 kilometers per second (186,282 miles per second).

Helium-4, when cooled to just above absolute zero, can flow without any viscosity, climbing walls and defying gravity.

Particles can become entangled, meaning the state of one instantly influences the state of another, regardless of distance.

Discovered in 2012, the Higgs boson is a particle that gives other particles their mass.

Simulated Large Hadron Collider CMS particle detector data depicting a Higgs boson produced by colliding protons decaying into hadron jets and electrons

Assuming the lambda-CDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter) model, a mathematical model of the Big Bang theory, is correct, dark energy makes up about 68% of the universe. This mysterious force is responsible for the accelerated expansion of the universe.

For every particle, there is an antiparticle with the same mass but opposite charge. When matter and antimatter meet, they annihilate each other. This principle is used in PET (Positron Emission Tomography) scans in medicine. Scientists claim that antimatter is the costliest material to make.

Superconductors are materials that can conduct electricity without resistance when cooled to very low temperatures, leading to potential revolutionary applications in technology.

Water is densest at 4°C (39°F), not at its freezing point. It expands as it cools below this temperature, which is why ice floats.

The Global Positioning System (GPS) must account for time dilation effects from both special and general relativity to provide accurate positioning.

Electrons and other particles exhibit properties of both waves and particles, challenging our classical understanding of physics.

Information about matter that falls into a black hole is seemingly lost, conflicting with quantum mechanics which states information must be conserved.

The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that entropy, or disorder, always increases over time in a closed system, which gives a direction to time. Unfortunately, this does not mean that sorting and straightening your books on the shelf constitutes time travel, because the shelf is not a closed system, and the energy you expend in the process (through bodily functions, generating heat, etc.) contributes to an overall increase in entropy in the environment.

When a particle travels through a medium faster than the speed of light in that medium, it emits a blue glow, known as Cherenkov radiation.

Solids feel solid because of the electromagnetic force; the electrons in atoms repel each other, preventing us from passing through solid objects.

The smallest meaningful length in physics is the Planck length (~1.6 x 10−35 meters), beyond which the concept of space breaks down due to quantum effects.

Two uncharged metallic plates placed close together in a vacuum can attract each other due to quantum vacuum fluctuations, known as the Casimir effect.

Muons, a type of elementary particle, live longer when traveling close to the speed of light due to time dilation, allowing them to reach Earth’s surface from the upper atmosphere.

Empty space is not truly empty but is filled with fleeting virtual particles that pop in and out of existence, contributing to the vacuum energy.

In certain systems, such as lasers, it is possible to have negative temperatures on the Kelvin scale, indicating a system hotter than any positive temperature.

The frequency of waves (sound, light) changes based on the relative motion of the source and the observer, explaining why a siren changes pitch as it passes by. This is known as the Doppler Effect.

Phase velocity is the rate at which a wave propagates in any medium. In some materials, the phase velocity of light can exceed the speed of light in a vacuum. However, this does not violate relativity because the speed of light in a medium is influenced by the refractive index of that medium. The refractive index determines how much the speed of light slows down when it enters a medium compared to its speed in vacuum, and it can be greater than 1. Despite this, the fundamental principle that no information or matter travels faster than light in a vacuum is still upheld.
