 Non-fiction Trivia
Biology

Octopuses have three hearts: Two pump blood to the gills, while the third pumps it to the rest of the body.

A blue whale’s heart is the size of a small car. It weighs about 1,300 pounds and can be heard beating from two miles away.

Some species of jellyfish are immortal. The Turritopsis dohrnii can revert to its juvenile form after reaching adulthood, theoretically allowing it to live indefinitely.

Bananas are berries, but strawberries aren’t. In botanical terms, a berry is a fruit produced from the ovary of a single flower with seeds embedded in the flesh.

A group of flamingos is called a “flamboyance”. These birds are known for their bright pink feathers and social nature.

Honey never spoils. Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that are over 3,000 years old and still edible.

Wombat poop is cube-shaped. This unique shape prevents it from rolling away and helps mark their territory effectively.

The fingerprints of a koala are virtually indistinguishable from human fingerprints. Even under a microscope, they are so similar that they could be confused at a crime scene.

The heart of a shrimp is located in its head. In shrimp, the heart and some of the other organs are in the cephalothorax, protected by a hard shell.

Sloths can hold their breath longer than dolphins. Sloths can slow their heart rate and hold their breath for up to 40 minutes underwater, while dolphins need to surface for air more frequently.

The human brain, while accounting for only about 2% of body weight, consumes roughly 20% of the body’s oxygen and calories.

Tardigrades, also known as water bears, can survive extreme conditions including temperatures as low as −458°F (−272°C, just one degree above absolute zero) and as high as 300°F (149°C), radiation, dehydration, and the vacuum of space.

Some species of starfish can regenerate entire limbs, and in some cases, an entire starfish can regenerate from a single limb. Planarian flatworms can regenerate their entire bodies from small tissue fragments, providing valuable insights into regenerative medicine.

The sea slug Elysia chlorotica can perform photosynthesis by incorporating genes from the algae it eats into its own cells. The spotted salamander is the only known vertebrate capable of photosynthesis, thanks to algae that live inside its cells.

Elephants have exceptional memories and can remember locations of water holes, paths, and even individuals over long periods.

The blue whale is the largest animal ever known to have lived on Earth, with individuals reaching up to 100 feet in length and weighing as much as 200 tons.

Electric eels can generate electric shocks of up to 600 volts to stun prey and defend against predators.

Some organisms, such as fireflies and certain species of jellyfish, can produce light through a chemical reaction called bioluminescence.

The platypus, one of the few venomous mammals, has spurs on its hind legs that can deliver a painful venom capable of incapacitating smaller animals.

Bacteria can communicate with each other using chemical signals in a process called quorum sensing, allowing them to coordinate behavior based on population density.

The mimic octopus can imitate the appearance and movements of more than fifteen different marine species, such as lionfish, flatfish, and sea snakes.

Humans share about 60% of their DNA with bananas, illustrating the common genetic heritage of all life on Earth.

Polar bear fur is not actually white but translucent; it appears white because it reflects visible light. They also have black skin underneath their fur to absorb and retain heat from the sun.

Bats use echolocation to navigate and hunt in the dark, emitting high-frequency sounds and listening for the echoes that bounce back from objects.

Some plants can communicate with each other through root exudates and airborne chemicals, warning nearby plants of herbivore attacks.

The Arctic tern has the longest migration of any bird, traveling from its Arctic breeding grounds to the Antarctic and back each year, covering about 25,000 miles.

The giant squid, once considered a myth, can grow up to 43 feet long and has the largest eyes in the animal kingdom, each up to a foot in diameter.

The cone snail, despite its slow movement, is a deadly predator with venom potent enough to kill humans. Its venom contains a complex cocktail of toxins.

The wood frog can survive being frozen solid during winter, thawing out and resuming normal activity in the spring.

Chameleons can change their skin color not just for camouflage but also to communicate with other chameleons and regulate their body temperature.

Clownfish have a symbiotic relationship with sea anemones, where the anemones provide protection with their stinging tentacles, and the clownfish provide food scraps.

Crows and ravens are among the smartest animals on Earth, capable of using tools, solving complex problems, and even recognizing human faces.

Praying mantises are the only insects known to have 3D vision, allowing them to accurately judge distances and strike prey with precision.

Coconut crabs are the largest terrestrial arthropods, with a leg span of up to three feet, and are known for their ability to crack open coconuts with their powerful claws.

The icefish, found in Antarctic waters, has transparent blood because it lacks hemoglobin, relying on the oxygen-rich cold water to absorb sufficient oxygen through its skin.
