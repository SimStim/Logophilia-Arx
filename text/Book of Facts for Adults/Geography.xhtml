 Non-fiction Trivia
Geography

Mount Everest, the highest peak on Earth, actually moves 4 centimeters (1.6 inches) northeast every year due to tectonic plate movements. Its height has also increased over time due to the Indian and Eurasian plates colliding.

The tallest tree in the world, Hyperion, a coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens), is located in California’s Redwood National Park and stands at 379.7 feet (115.7 meters) tall.

The Amazon River discharges more freshwater into the ocean than the next seven largest rivers combined.

The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench is the deepest known point in the Earth’s oceans, reaching a depth of about 36,070 feet (10,994 meters).

Despite both being cold and icy, the Arctic and Antarctic differ significantly. The Arctic is an ocean surrounded by land, while Antarctica is a continent surrounded by ocean.

The Sahara Desert expands by about 1 kilometer (0.6 miles) southward each year due to desertification, which is caused by a combination of natural climate cycles and human activities.

Canada has the longest coastline of any country in the world, measuring approximately 202,080 kilometers (125,570 miles) including its islands.

Madrid, Spain, and Wellington, New Zealand, are antipodal cities, meaning they are almost exact opposites on the globe. If you were to dig straight through the Earth from one, you would come out near the other.

Finland is often called the “Land of a Thousand Lakes,” but it actually has over 188,000 lakes within its borders.

Mawsynram, a village in Meghalaya, India, holds the record for being the wettest place on Earth, with an average annual rainfall of around 467.4 inches (11,871 millimeters).

The Antarctic Desert is the largest desert in the world, covering about 14 million square kilometers, as it is classified as a cold desert.

Point Nemo is the oceanic pole of inaccessibility, the farthest point from any land in the world, located in the South Pacific Ocean.

Over 60% of the world’s freshwater is contained in just 10 lakes, with Lake Baikal in Russia holding about 20% alone.

The Haskell Free Library and Opera House sits on the border of the USA and Canada, with the border running through the building.

The summit of Mount Chimborazo in Ecuador is the point on Earth’s surface farthest from the Earth’s center due to the equatorial bulge.

The shortest place name is “Å,” which is used by several villages in Sweden and Norway.

All continents’ names in English end with the same letter they start with: Asia, Africa, Europe, America, Antarctica, and Australia.

Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, both by area (44 hectares) and population (around 800 people).

Despite its name, Greenland is largely covered by ice, while Iceland, though named for its icy coasts, is relatively green.

China, despite its vast size, only has one official time zone (China Standard Time).

Kazakhstan is the largest landlocked country and also has the largest lake, the Caspian Sea, which is actually a saltwater lake with no outlet.

Angel Falls in Venezuela is the world’s highest uninterrupted waterfall, plunging 979 meters (3,212 feet).

India has the world’s only floating post office on Dal Lake in Srinagar.

Bolivia has two capital cities: Sucre (constitutional capital) and La Paz (seat of government and executive capital).

Tokyo–Yokohama is considered the world’s largest metropolitan area, with a population exceeding 37 million people.

Manitoulin Island in Lake Huron, Canada, is the largest island in a freshwater lake in the world.

The full name of Taumata­whakata­ngihanga­koauau­otama­teapo­kaiwhe­nuaki­tana­tahu in New Zealand is one of the longest place names in the world.

Derinkuyu in Turkey is an ancient multi-level underground city large enough to house 20,000 people.

The Ganges–Brahmaputra Delta is the world’s largest delta, spanning more than 105,000 square kilometers.

The Great Wall of China, spanning approximately 4,500 miles (7,200 kilometers), is often believed to be visible from space, but this is a myth. Due to its construction materials, it’s not easily discernible with the naked eye from low orbit. In reality, spotting the wall from space requires specific weather and lighting conditions.

Lake Baikal in Siberia is the world’s deepest and oldest freshwater lake, reaching depths of 1,642 meters (5,387 feet).

The equator passes through 13 countries: Ecuador, Colombia, Brazil, Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Kenya, Somalia, São Tomé and Príncipe, Indonesia, Kiribati, and the Maldives.
