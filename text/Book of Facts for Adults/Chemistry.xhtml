 Non-fiction Trivia
Chemistry

Helium is the only element that was first discovered in space before being found on Earth. It was detected in the sun’s spectrum in 1868.

Unlike most substances, water expands as it freezes, making ice less dense than liquid water, which is why ice floats.

Oxygen was discovered independently by two scientists: Carl Wilhelm Scheele in Sweden and Joseph Priestley in England, both in the 1770s.

Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. It is sometimes called quicksilver.

Antoine Lavoisier, the father of modern chemistry, was executed by guillotine during the French Revolution.

Table salt (sodium chloride) is composed of two highly reactive elements: sodium, which reacts explosively with water, and chlorine, a poisonous gas.

Astatine is the rarest naturally occurring element in the Earth’s crust, with less than 1 gram present at any time.

The only letter not appearing on the periodic table is the letter J.

Gallium can melt in your hand because its melting point is around 29.76°C (85.57°F).

The smell of fresh-cut grass is a plant distress call. When damaged, plants release chemicals called green leaf volatiles (GLVs).

Marie Curie was the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields: Physics and Chemistry.

Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe, making up roughly 75% of its elemental mass.

The word “alcohol” is derived from the Arabic word “al-kuḥl”.

Phosphorus was discovered by Hennig Brandt in 1669 while he was attempting to create gold from urine.

Liquid nitrogen is so cold that it can freeze skin on contact, causing frostbite almost instantly.

Silicon, the primary component in sand, is the second most abundant element in the Earth’s crust.

The element krypton was discovered in 1898 by William Ramsay and Morris Travers in residue left after liquid air had nearly boiled away.

Helium is so light and non-reactive that it can escape the Earth’s gravitational pull and drift off into space.

Pure gold (24-karat) is so soft it can be molded by hand.

Carbon is the basis of all known life and exists in various forms, including diamond, graphite, and graphene. They have vastly different properties due to their different structures.

Different elements produce different colors in fireworks. For example, strontium yields red, and copper produces blue.

Bananas are slightly radioactive. They contain potassium-40, a radioactive isotope of potassium.

Liquid oxygen is attracted to magnets due to its paramagnetic properties.

Heavy water (D2O) is water where hydrogen atoms are replaced with deuterium, an isotope of hydrogen. It’s used in some nuclear reactors.

Fluorine is the most reactive element, capable of forming compounds with almost all other elements.

The smell of rain is called petrichor, caused by a compound called geosmin produced by soil bacteria.

Antibubbles are the opposite of bubbles; they are droplets of liquid surrounded by a thin film of gas.

Top left: normal bubbles on the surface of soapy water; right: antibubbles and submerged air bubbles within the largest of the antibubbles

Glass is technically a supercooled liquid or an amorphous solid, meaning it lacks a crystalline structure.

Dry ice, solid carbon dioxide, sublimates directly into gas without becoming liquid.

Phosphorescent materials glow in the dark after the light source is removed, unlike fluorescent materials which glow only under UV light.

Emotional tears contain more protein-based hormones than tears from irritants, like onions.

Theobromine, a compound found in chocolate, is toxic to dogs and some other animals.

There are more molecules in a single drop of water than there are stars in the Milky Way galaxy.

Rust is iron oxide (Fe2O3), formed when iron reacts with oxygen and water.

UV light can cause thymine dimers in DNA, which can lead to mutations and skin cancer.

There are seven diatomic elements that naturally occur as molecules: hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine, chlorine, bromine, and iodine.

Caffeine evolved as a natural pesticide in coffee plants, deterring insects from eating the leaves.
