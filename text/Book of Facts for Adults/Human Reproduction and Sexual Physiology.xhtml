Human Reproduction and Sexual Physiology

The average speed of male ejaculation is around 28 miles per hour (45 km/h).

After a man goes through puberty, each of his testicles will begin producing around 100 million sperm every day, which is approximately 2,000 in total every second, about 6 billion each month, or nearly 6 trillion over an average lifespan.

Sperm can survive within the female reproductive system for up to a week. However, this is more likely if intercourse occurs during the fertile part of the menstrual cycle, around the time the egg is released. The average lifespan of sperm cells is about 3 days.

During male orgasm, several hormones and neurochemicals are released: oxytocin, dopamine, prolactin, serotonin, noradrenaline, endorphins, testosterone, vasopressin, nitric oxide, adrenaline, acetylcholine, endocannabinoids, and luteinizing hormone. Yes, it’s complicated!

Women can have different kinds of orgasms: clitoral, vaginal, mixed clitoral/vaginal, whole body, anal, non-genital, cervical, and G-area.

The clitoris contains 2.5 times as many nerve endings as the penis, making it highly sensitive.

Testosterone levels in men are highest in the morning.

The G-spot is named after the German physician and scientist Ernst Gräfenberg.

Orgasms can alleviate menstrual cramps due to the release of endorphins.

The smell of male sweat can increase female arousal due to pheromones like andrastadienone, boosting levels of the hormone cortisol in women who sniff it.

In rare cases, men can experience spontaneous orgasms unrelated to sexual arousal or stimulation.

Female orgasm can increase the acidity in the reproductive tract, potentially enhancing sperm motility.

Testosterone levels in men can influence their voice pitch and body odor.

Lower-pitched voices in men are rated by women as attractive and masculine. However, studies have shown that men with attractive voices do not have better semen quality. In fact, men whose voices are rated as more attractive tend to have lower concentrations of sperm in their ejaculate.

The combined scent of lavender and pumpkin pie has been found to increase penile blood flow by approximately 40%, more than any other scent.

Typically, the right testicle is larger than the left. Additionally, one testicle (usually the left) often hangs lower than the other to avoid collisions and maintain optimal temperature.

Research published in the journal Intelligence shows that the smarter the man, the higher the quality of his sperm.

Women who take oral contraceptives tend to prefer partners with different genetic compatibility than when they are not taking them.

Dysorgasmia refers to the experience of painful orgasms, typically felt in the abdomen. This condition can occur during or after orgasm, and sometimes even several hours later. Both men and women can experience this type of pain.

The shape of the uterus can affect fertility and the likelihood of successful implantation of a fertilized egg.

Sexsomnia, or sleep sex, is a specific type of parasomnia—an abnormal activity occurring during sleep. It is characterized by engaging in sexual acts while in non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep.

Testosterone levels in men decline gradually with age. The average drop is about 1% per year after age 30.

The clitoris is the only organ in the human body that exists solely for pleasure.

The average size of a flaccid penis is around 3.5 inches (9 cm), and erect, it’s about 5.1 inches (13 cm).

The hymen is a thin membrane that can vary greatly in elasticity and may not necessarily tear during first intercourse.

Breast size can fluctuate throughout a woman’s menstrual cycle due to hormonal changes. Estrogen levels increase before a period, causing the milk ducts and glands to swell.

Kegel exercises, which strengthen pelvic floor muscles, can enhance sexual arousal and lubrication.

The uterus expands significantly during pregnancy, growing from about the size of a lemon to that of a watermelon.

During arousal, the vaginal canal lengthens to accommodate different penis sizes. Sexual arousal causes the cervix and uterus to lift up and out of the way, which results in the upper two-thirds of the vagina lengthening.

Breastfeeding can suppress ovulation and menstruation due to hormonal changes. The more frequent and longer the breastfeeding sessions, the extended the period of anovulation and infertility will be.

Semen is primarily composed of water, plasma, and mucus. It contains 5 to 25 calories and over 200 different proteins, vitamins, and minerals, including calcium, citrate, fructose, glucose, lactic acid, magnesium, potassium, protein, and zinc. Each ejaculation typically contains 200 to 300 million sperm.

Sperm quality can be influenced by lifestyle factors such as diet, obesity, caffeine consumption, smoking, and alcohol and drug use.

The brain’s hypothalamus increases sexual activity and emotional behavior, which can consequently lead to reactions of violent anger.

Testosterone is the primary male hormone, but females also require it for healthy body functioning, contributing to libido and muscle mass. Females typically have much lower testosterone levels than males. During menopause, estrogen levels typically drop, which can make the male hormones, or androgens, relatively higher.

The refractory period after male ejaculation is typically longer than that of females, during which further arousal is difficult.

A woman is born with 1–2 million eggs, which are all she will ever have in her lifetime. The number of immature egg cells, or oocytes, decreases by about 11,000 each month before puberty. By the time she reaches puberty, she has around 300,000 to 400,000 eggs. By the age of 32, more than 90% of her eggs will have died off. Ultimately, only about 300–400 of these eggs will be ovulated.
