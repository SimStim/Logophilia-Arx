 Non-fiction Trivia
Astronomy and Space

The Sun makes up about 99.86% of the mass of the entire solar system.

Venus is the hottest planet in our solar system with surface temperatures reaching up to 900°F (475°C), hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun.

Neutron stars are so dense that a sugar-cube-sized amount of their material would weigh about a billion tons on Earth.

Jupiter has the shortest day of all the planets, completing a rotation on its axis in just about 10 hours.

One day on Mercury is equivalent to 176 Earth days due to its slow rotation and fast orbit around the Sun.

There are more stars in the universe than grains of sand on all the beaches on Earth, with estimates ranging up to 1 septillion (1,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000) stars.

The Milky Way galaxy is on a collision course with the Andromeda galaxy and they are expected to merge in about 4.5 billion years.

Black holes are regions of space where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape from them, hence their name.

The moon is drifting away from Earth at a rate of about 1.5 inches (3.8 cm) per year.

There is a giant water vapor cloud in space, containing 140 trillion times more water than all of Earth’s oceans combined, located around a quasar called APM 08279+5255.

Saturn’s moon Enceladus has geysers that spew water and organic materials, suggesting the potential for life beneath its icy crust.

A day on Mars is only slightly longer than a day on Earth, with a Martian day (or sol) lasting 24 hours and 39 minutes.

There are more trees on Earth than stars in the Milky Way galaxy. Earth has about 3 trillion trees compared to the Milky Way’s 100–400 billion stars. Let’s all make a concerted effort to ensure this remains true for a long time.

Uranus rotates on its side, with its axis tilted by about 98 degrees, possibly due to a massive collision in its past.

The largest volcano in the solar system is Olympus Mons on Mars, which stands about 13.6 miles (22 km) high—nearly three times the height of Mount Everest.

The Great Red Spot on Jupiter is a giant storm that has been raging for at least 400 years and is larger than the entire Earth.

Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is the farthest human-made object from Earth, having entered interstellar space in 2012.

A year on Neptune is equivalent to 165 Earth years due to its vast distance from the Sun.

Pluto is smaller in diameter than the United States, with a diameter of about 1,473 miles (2,370 km) compared to the U.S.’s width of about 2,680 miles (4,320 km).

If two pieces of the same type of metal touch in space, they will bond together permanently. This process is called cold welding and occurs because the atoms have no way of knowing they are in separate pieces of metal.

Interstellar space is not completely empty. It contains a few atoms per cubic centimeter, primarily hydrogen and helium, along with dust particles and cosmic rays. Interstellar space begins at the heliopause, the boundary where the Sun’s solar wind is stopped by the interstellar medium. This marks the end of the solar system and the beginning of interstellar space.

Cosmic rays, which are high-energy particles originating from outside the solar system, constantly bombard interstellar space. These rays pose a significant hazard to spacecraft and future interstellar travelers.

The Oort Cloud is a hypothesized shell of icy objects that exist in the outermost reaches of the solar system, nearly a light-year from the Sun. It represents the boundary between the solar system and interstellar space.

Barnard’s Star, located about 5.96 light-years away, is the closest single star to the Sun. It is a red dwarf and has been the subject of much study due to its proximity.

The Local Interstellar Cloud (LIC) is a region of space about 30 light-years across, through which our solar system is currently moving. It is composed of warm, sparse interstellar material.

The galaxy MCG+01-02-015 is one of the loneliest galaxies in the universe. It’s located in the Pisces constellation and is 100 million light-years away from the nearest galaxy.

The most precise natural clock in the universe is the pulsar PSR B1937+21, which spins 642 times per second, providing incredibly accurate time measurements.

It takes the Solar System about 225–250 million years to complete one orbit around the Milky Way’s center. This period is known as a “galactic year” or “cosmic year.”

Our Milky Way galaxy is moving towards a mysterious region of space called the Great Attractor, which has a gravitational pull so strong that it is pulling galaxies towards it.

The Boomerang Nebula is the coldest known natural place in the universe, with a temperature of 1 K (−272.15°C), just one degree above absolute zero.

Neptune has a layer of “hot ice.” Due to intense pressure, water exists in a state where it’s both solid and incredibly hot.

At the center of most galaxies, including our Milky Way, there is a supermassive black hole. The one in our galaxy, Sagittarius A*, has a mass of about 4 million suns.

There is a planet called 55 Cancri e that is likely composed largely of carbon and may have a surface made of diamond.

Time is relative and can be affected by speed and gravity. Astronauts aboard the International Space Station experience time slightly faster than people on Earth due to the weaker gravitational field.

There are potentially billions of rogue planets in our galaxy alone, wandering through space without a star to orbit.

Located in the Eagle Nebula (M16), the Pillars of Creation are towering columns of gas and dust where new stars are forming. The iconic Hubble Space Telescope image shows these structures in stunning detail.

Hypervelocity stars are stars that travel at speeds exceeding 1 million miles per hour (1.6 million km/h). They are thought to be ejected from their home galaxies due to interactions with supermassive black holes.

The Moon experiences moonquakes, which are caused by the gravitational interactions with Earth. Unlike Earthquakes, moonquakes can last for up to an hour due to the Moon’s lack of liquid core to dampen vibrations.

In the atmospheres of planets like Saturn and Jupiter, it rains diamonds. High pressure and temperature conditions cause carbon atoms to crystallize into diamonds that then rain down into the planet’s interior.

Gravitational lensing occurs when massive objects like galaxies bend light around them due to their gravitational pull. This effect allows astronomers to observe distant objects that would otherwise be too faint to detect.

Some stars are known as “cannibal stars” because they consume their companion stars. These interactions can lead to dramatic events like supernovae or the formation of exotic stellar remnants.

Large galaxies sometimes devour smaller galaxies in a process known as galactic cannibalism. The Milky Way has cannibalized several smaller galaxies throughout its history, incorporating their stars and gas.
