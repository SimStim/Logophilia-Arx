Terms of Surrender

by Idoru Toei

‘accusation.’ “Villainy.” ‘accusation.’ “Theft!”

“Actually, you’ll find that I am quite devoted to law and order, counselor. It is, after all, my practice,” said the human on the nexor carrying a photo-visual connection across the void of the heliosphere, her features washed pale by the screen’s glow and arranged, of all things, into an expression of quiet hurt.

The invasion, it was becoming clear, was not going as planned.

Earlier…

The invasion-class battleship Zorath was hot-warping toward an unexplored arm of the galaxy, the light of familiar stars smearing past the viewports in long, luminous threads. The ship hummed around its crew, a deep vibration to be felt in the deck plates and in the bones of the hand, while the recycled air carried its faint, familiar bite of ozone. Hot-warping was expensive; for a vessel the size of Zorath, the resource costs grew truly absurd.

But spies among the fleeing Morvath had reported back that they had made contact with a planet of sophonts in this very region. A treasure, ready for the taking! And unlike the Morvath, these new people lacked faster-than-light travel, and so could not flee the expanding Empire.

Whichever faction staked a claim on the planet first stood to benefit tremendously, and so the Bhorgal Collective was sparing no expense. The cost of the hot-warp would be recouped 100-fold by the cultural resources of ‘Earth.’

“Preparation for exiting hot-warp is underway,” technical officer Qorlan informed the admiral. A moment later a tremor ran through the ship, stern to bow, faint but unmistakable. “Hot-warp: successful. This vessel has reached their heliosphere. Fourteen klakons remain until final destination.”

“Satisfactory. When the remainder of the fleet materializes, syncing of controls to the system of the Collective will occur. Preparations for entry will follow.” Admiral Kharvok settled back, pleased. “Scanning for competitors and Morvath remnants will occur.”

“Admiral,” objected Urr, the protocol officer, “chasing of the Morvath is not within the assigned tasking of this fleet.”

“Counts read zero for both,” military advisor Drazgor reported. “Apparent event: the Morvath fleet cold-warped out as soon as recharging completed.”

“The waste is regrettable,” Kharvok said with a wry smile. Cold-warps take a long time; if any warp remnants still drifted in this system, the Bhorgal Collective could hot-warp a fleet to wait for the Morvath at their destination. “Assessment: excess greed is inefficient. A single planet and its billions of fresh citizens will satisfy the Collective this time.”

“Analysis of broadcasts from the target is underway,” said Qorlan. “The universal translator will be trained and deployment-ready by the time of arrival.”

“Scanning of the target with long-range sensors is in progress. A minimally-damaging invasion plan will be ready at that time,” said Drazgor in the flat, unhurried tone of deep routine. This was not their first invasion. “Per existing intel, the Earth holds nothing capable of damaging the Zorath.”

“Excellent.” Pleasure settled more deeply in Kharvok’s chest. His crew was performing like a well-trained squad, exactly as expected.

This invasion, Kharvok knew, would go off without a hitch.

Almost fourteen klakons later…

A hitch appeared.

The bridge had settled into the hush of final approach: the low murmur of consoles, the viewports bright with a single swelling point of light, the faint tremble of the deck beneath their feet. “An anomaly,” said Qorlan. “Galnet node detected nearby.” The Galnet belonged to no single Empire, which was precisely why almost every spacefaring society had come to rely on its quiet convenience.

“Abandonment by the Morvath?” speculated Urr. “Convenience results for this fleet. If the node is held in the hands of the leader of this world, perhaps negotiation of their complete surrender can occur without damage to their infrastructure.”

“Universal translator: ready?”

“Online. Completion holds at 98%. The Earth language is surprisingly partitioned such that—”

“Status of invasion plans?” Kharvok interrupted.

“Drafted and ready. Estimate: destruction of target below 10%. Although the optimal case remains: no shot is fired by any party.”

“Communications will open,” ordered Kharvok.

For 10% destruction averted is 10% more profit for the Bhorgal Collective.

Moments later an Earthling appeared on the ship’s nexor screen, bright and sharp against the bridge’s low light.

“Well howdy! More visitors to Earth? Must be my lucky day. I wasn’t expecting walk-in clients!”

“Confirmed. Surrender, obviously.” Kharvok saw no reason to arrange the words with more care.

“I beg your pardon? For the record, was that an offer, or an ultimatum?” The Earthling scratched the fur on her head.

Urr stepped in for a moment. “Does the representative hold qualification to speak for the planet of origin?”

The Earthling laughed. “I reckon I am. Attorney of record for this planet—all of it—admitted pro hac vice.”

Urr gave Kharvok an ‘all-good’ signal and stepped away.

Kharvok continued, “Then, in the capacity of the Earth’s representative—”

“Van Manderpootz will do. Angelica van Manderpootz, attorney at law. Call me Angelica.”

The Earthling’s designation was irrelevant to Kharvok, and he let it pass unacknowledged, the way one ignores furniture.

“Declaration: this event is an invasion. No defensive chance exists against this fleet. Complete surrender minimizes the suffering of the Earth-party. The people and resources of the Earth will be allocated to roles decided by the Collective. The Earth-party holds no decision-input in this matter, but killing will not occur. This is the optimal available choice. There remain 82 klikons available for deciding.”

“Oh, I can decide right now. No, my client respectfully declines, without prejudice. Thank you for asking, though.”

Kharvok paused. There was a chance, he told himself, that the translation was simply not landing correctly. “Assessment: certainty is absent that the situation is understood by the Earth-party. This fleet is currently approaching the planet of origin…”

“Oh, yes! I can see that. Terribly handy device, this nexor-tech. Part space-phone, part Encyclopedia Galactica, and it can show me anything any other node can see, in real-time! It let me know you were coming the moment you left Gorbath. That’s your home planet, right? Gorbath? Discovery, counselor, is a wonderful thing. Terribly handy device…”

“… Confirmed. And as is visible to the Earth-party, arrival at the destination will occur shortly. And under close observation, the Earth-party will notice that this fleet consists of very many, very heavily armed, ships. At arrival, if the Earth-party has not surrendered by choice, surrender will be forced. With overwhelming force. Due to the numerous heavily armed ships. Does this translation reach the Earth-party successfully?”

The Earthling scratched her head again and bared her teeth in a fashion that the translating computer rendered, with clinical precision, as a ‘bashful smile.’ “No, the fault is mine … I’m burying the lede. You cannot invade Earth. As a matter of law. Is that more clear?”

Another pause. “This fleet holds many many ships. And the ships hold great big guns. Objectively, invasion of the Earth can occur. And it is occurring, at present. That is the currently occurring event. Is the Earth-party certain of successful receipt of this translation?” Kharvok felt the first faint pressure of frustration behind his sternum. It was a rare sensation for him.

“My fault again,” the Earthling offered, ‘bashful smile’ firmly in place. “Invading Earth is illegal. Black-letter law. Does that help?”

“Ah,” said Kharvok, as understanding finally dawned. “False. The laws of the Earth carry no relevance in this matter. Invaders set the rules; this is standard. Law functions only when force backs it. And this fleet, the invader, holds the overwhelming force. Is the matter understood? The Bhorgal Collective, one of the largest factions of the Mordravonan Empire, is present here, and this fleet operates in its representation. Assuredly the Morvath acquaintances of the Earth-party mentioned the Collective. Constraint of the Collective by Earth legality: for what reason would that occur? Nonsense. The Collective holds great big guns. The guns of the Earth-party are not as big. Rendering of this matter in simpler form is not possible.”

On the screen, the Earthling’s face arranged itself into a ‘frown.’ “Again I have failed to make myself plain. Invading Earth is illegal under the Mordravonan Empire’s own rules. See, e.g., Odravak Collective v. Korrath Syndicate, 112 Red. Imp. 447 (2198): holding incursions into registered systems void ab initio, force notwithstanding. You said you are part of the Empire, right? My understanding is that you gentlemen love to follow the rules.”

Kharvok glanced at Urr, then back at the nexor display. “The Earth-party is mistaken. Invasion of unincorporated systems violates no rule of the Empire.”

“Oh, well … there’s your misunderstanding. This system is already a part of the Mordravonan Empire. On the rolls, in the register, a matter of public record.” The smile returned, broad and untroubled.

“That…” The pressure behind Kharvok’s sternum climbed another notch. Was this Earthling being intentionally foolish? “That outcome is not possible. The ships of this fleet are the first of the Empire to reach this system. Therefore, membership in the Empire is not yet held by the Earth. But … acquisition of membership will occur soon.”

The Earthling looked down at the nexor controls in front of her, ‘concentration’ written in every line of her face. “Here, this might help. I apologize again; this system is still new to me, and I do hate procedural error. Don’t worry though, I’m a fast learner.”

“Admiral!” Qorlan exclaimed.

On the ship’s internal display, the planet had acquired a new tag: ‘Mordravonan Empire, Odravak Collective’.

“Halt of the battle computers has occurred,” Drazgor announced.

One could not, after all, target a friendly system for invasion.

Kharvok looked meaningfully at Urr, who was furiously manipulating his station’s controls. How could this be happening?

Returning to the node screen, Kharvok said, “The Earth-party holds no membership in the Empire. The Earth-party hacked the nexor. This will only delay the inevitable. The Earth-party will surrender, or the Earth-party will be subjugated. There exists no other possible outcome.”

“Objection: assumes facts not in evidence. No hack was necessary. This system, this planet, and, for the avoidance of doubt, myself as well: all duly registered parts of the Odravak Collective, and therefore of the Empire. My condolences on the voyage; all this way for nothing. That was probably expensive.” ‘smug.’

A distraught Urr turned to the admiral. ‘True,’ he signaled.

The expenditure of the hot-warp alone would set the Bhorgal Collective back a bit. Competition among collectives was a fierce and patient thing, and a setback like this might provoke one of its rivals into attacking, weakening the Collective further.

Collectives had fallen for less in the past.

Kharvok’s eyes flicked toward Qorlan. If they could override the friendly-fire lockout and take this planet anyway…

“I hope you’re not thinking of invading a friendly planet,” the Earthling said. “As your counsel, I must advise against it. The Empire’s military frowns on that sort of thing. You know, the Mordravonan Empire’s planet-conquering military? Tend to discourage fighting within the Empire, via overwhelming force? Great big guns are held by the military of the Empire. The guns of the Bhorgal fleet are not as big. Rendering of this matter in simpler form is not possible.”

A long pause. “By what mechanism?”

“I really was hoping you’d ask,” the Earthling said, ‘smug’ rendered to perfection. “Are you familiar with Rock-Paper-Scissors?”

Urr piped up, “An Earth game played by arranging their appendages in specific configurations. Selection of the winner appears to occur per random chance.”

“There you go.” ‘satisfaction.’ “The exhibit speaks for itself.”

A longer pause followed, and still the Earthling said nothing. “Further explanation is required.” Kharvok’s frustration had reached an unprecedented level.

“Oh, sure. You’re right that my friends the Morvath did in fact mention you. The Mordravonan Empire, the various factions—it was all very instructive. They gave us a Galnet node and instructed us on its use. They were very sorry, you see, to discover intelligent life in a system they were passing through. Inevitably, the Empire would follow, and Earth would be lost. Sad, but inevitable. Foreseeable, too ... and foreseeability, counselor, is half the law.”

So far, this squared with Kharvok’s understanding of the situation. This was not the first time something of the kind had happened in the Empire’s long history.

“Inevitable,” she continued, “but unacceptable. The question before us was one of remedies. We had a space-phone and an encyclopedia of galactic knowledge, but what we didn’t have was time. No time to research technology, no time to prepare the military, no time for any real preventative measures, not nearly enough. Pretty soon, one of the factions would get wind of our existence and head our way. And our time would be up. What we did then would be determined by who exactly was coming. Which turned out to be you.

“Ships coming from Gorbath. A whole fleet, with lots of guns. Gorbath is a planet in the Bhorgal Collective. So we opened negotiations with your friendly rivals, the Odravak. The consideration: pointlessly wasting your resources and weakening the Bhorgal Collective. In exchange, they grant us essentially autonomy while being in-name part of their Collective.”

“False,” Kharvok objected, “no collective would bargain with an outsider system, especially in return for nothing. The cost to the Bhorgal Collective is a fraction of the worth of this system.”

“Oh, we were already part of the Empire. They couldn’t stake their claim on us anyway, since fighting is not allowed between Empire systems. Putting us in-name-only dissuades you from invading and allows them a legal claim for reparations from Bhorgal if you decide to invade us anyway. It cost them nothing, and might pay off big, if you’re stupid. I reviewed the instrument very carefully. I am a lawyer aftera—”

“The Earth already held membership in the Empire by what mechanism?” Behind Kharvok’s sternum, his lifelong record for frustration was quietly surpassed yet again.

“Did I not mention?” she tilted her head. “Technically I suppose we’re a Ghorv colony.”

“The Ghorv…” This made even less sense. “Xenophobes. Even if the Earth-party attempted contact through Galnet, failure would result.”

“Yes,” the Earthling said, nodding, “that’s why they have vassal races to deal with outsiders. One of those vassals happens to be the Kharostians; retained, I might add, for precisely this kind of matter!” ‘proud.’

Another pause. Were Earthlings telepathic? This ‘Angelica’ appeared to believe it was transmitting more information than was actually being received. And what, exactly, was the significance of the warrior-race Kharostians? In the end, Kharvok had no choice but to prod: “And?”

“Oh, I’m Kharostian. By operation of law.” ‘confused.’ “Did I forget to mention that, too?”

Kharvok stared at the monitor. “The Earth-party is not Kharostian. The Earth-party is clearly Earthling.”

“Human,” said the Earthling. “Yes, I was born a human, but I am now Kharostian … for all legal intents and purposes.”

If frustration could burst a body from within, the bridge of the Zorath would already have been covered in gore. “By what mechanism?” Kharvok raged.

“Adoption. You know how Kharostians capture defeated enemies and raise them as their own? I contacted a Kharostian family through the Galnet and challenged them to Rock-Paper-Scissors. It took a few matches, but I eventually lost: a duly witnessed, best-of series with no grounds for appeal. My poor foster-father, he was so confused as to why I insisted he adopt me over a simple game. Although, I guess it might have helped that I own a planet. Having a daughter with her own planet will certainly make things easier when the family comes to visit.”

“Of course the Earth-party owns a planet.” Kharvok had passed beyond frustration and was now, with a strange calm, entering mania. “For what reason would this not be so?! The whole planet? This makes exactly as much sense as the rest of this conversation!”

The human laughed. “Yeah, you think negotiating my way through the Mordravonan Empire was tough? Trying to get all this world’s leaders to agree on pizza toppings was harder. Having an invasion fleet on the way sure helped things along though, so thank you. Since Earth surrendered to me, and I’m Kharostian, and Kharostians are vassals of the Ghorv, and the Ghorv are part of the Empire ... the chain of title, gentlemen, is airtight.”

A second human appeared next to the nexor.

“Ms. van Manderpootz, we’re ready.”

“Excellent! I believe the record will reflect that I’m done delaying you gentlemen.”

A panicked Qorlan suddenly exclaimed, “Admiral! The systems of this vessel have entered remote mode. Lockout of this technical officer has occurred!”

“Equivalent lockout holds for this military advisor,” confirmed Drazgor.

Urr said nothing at all, but the worried grunts he made while trying his own console, to no effect, carried their own report.

“Relax, fellas,” the human said. “You’re in no danger. Negotiations have concluded, successfully. The instruments have been executed.”

Kharvok, somewhere along the way, had quietly gone cross-eyed.

Urr demanded, “Negotiations between which parties, concerning what?”

“Oh, negotiations between the Odravak and Bhorgal. For, uh, I guess possession of your fleet? See, because this is a private star system that you are currently trespassing upon, and the posted schedule of charges provides ‘parking fees’ for any vessel remaining within the heliosphere. See Bhorgal Collective v. Draugor Combine, 74 Jeip. 312 (2199): enforcing per-vessel assessments against flag collectives.”

“Parking fees?” Urr was incensed. “No such instrument exists within the knowledge of this protocol officer!”

The human waggled a finger. “Ignorantia legis neminem excusat—ignorance of the law is no excuse. You should have looked up the rules before your visit.”

“Quantification of the fees is requested.” Urr demanded.

“Exorbitant,” the human said, as though the word were an explanation complete in itself. “Per vessel, too, and you brought very many. So many that I’m not sure even the whole Bhorgal Collective could bear the cost. They’re the ones truly liable, right? Didn’t you say—and I quote—‘The Bhorgal Collective, one of the largest factions of the Mordravonan Empire, is present here, and this fleet operates in its representation’?”

Nobody on board the Zorath could speak.

“Well, we gave the Bhorgal an election of remedies: pay the fees they were legally required to pay, or surrender the fleet. And the crews. Which they did.”

Drazgor closed his eyes.

It was a complete defeat, and not a single shot had been fired.

“But, as I said at the outset, quoting your own proposed terms, which I found quite reasonable,” continued the human, “don’t worry. Your people and resources will be allocated to roles decided by Earth executives. You hold no decision-input in this matter, but killing will not occur.”

The fleet turned as one, obedient and silent, and began the long remote-controlled drift toward the planet. Kharvok started laughing.

He never stopped.
